Field Guide: Greatest Common Factor

1. List and match

18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24

The biggest number in both lists is the GCF: 6.

2. The "split again" check

Think 2 is the GCF of 24 and 36? Divide: 24 ÷ 2 = 12, 36 ÷ 2 = 18.

12 and 18 still share 6, so 2 wasn't the greatest. When the leftovers share only 1, you've got it.

3. Quick rules

• If the smaller number divides the bigger one, the smaller one is the GCF (GCF(6, 18) = 6).

• The GCF is never bigger than the smaller number.

• Numbers next to each other (20, 21) only share 1.

4. Real-world jobs

• Most identical groups with nothing left over → GCF.

• Largest square tile that fits a rectangle → GCF of length and width.

• Factor a sum: 24 + 36 = 12(2 + 3), since 12 × 2 = 24 and 12 × 3 = 36.
