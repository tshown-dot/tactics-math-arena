Field Guide: Greatest Common Factor

1. The Factor Pairs (Rainbow) Method

A factor is a whole number that divides another number evenly with zero remainder. Factors always come in pairs: $1 \times 24 = 24$, $2 \times 12 = 24$, $3 \times 8 = 24$, $4 \times 6 = 24$.

2. Direct Multiple Rule

If number A divides number B evenly (for example, $4$ divides $12$), then the smaller number ($4$) is automatically the Greatest Common Factor!

3. Co-Prime Numbers

Two numbers that share NO common factor other than 1 are called co-prime (e.g. 15 and 16, or 20 and 21). Consecutive integers are always co-prime, and their GCF is strictly 1.

4. Common Factor vs. GREATEST Common Factor

Two numbers may share multiple common factors (for example, 18 and 24 share 1, 2, 3, and 6). While 2 and 3 are shared factors, only 6 is the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) capable of generating 100% torque!
