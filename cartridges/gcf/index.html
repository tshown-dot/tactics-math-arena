Field Guide: Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

1. Factor Pairs & Factor Rainbows

Factors are whole numbers that multiply together to make a target number with zero remainder. Every factor has a partner: for $24$, the pairs are $1 \times 24$, $2 \times 12$, $3 \times 8$, and $4 \times 6$.

2. Common Factors vs. GREATEST Common Factor

Any factor that appears on both Dial A and Dial B is a Common Factor. For $18$ and $24$, the common factors are $1, 2, 3,$ and $6$. Only the largest one ($6$) is the Greatest Common Factor (GCF)!

3. Factoring with the GCF (Distributive Property)

When you divide both numbers by their GCF, the remaining parts have no common factor other than $1$: $18 + 24 = \mathbf{6}(3 + 4)$.

4. Co-Prime Trap Vaults

Consecutive numbers (like $15$ and $16$) share no factor other than $1$! They are called co-prime, and their GCF is always $1$.
